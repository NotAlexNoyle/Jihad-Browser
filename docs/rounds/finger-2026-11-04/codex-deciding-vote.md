PICK: 2

Enable the engine switch, leave the daemon switch OFF, and move on without building the scaler. This captures a demonstrated benefit while avoiding another speculative implementation cycle. It leaves Wikipedia’s R3 target unmet.

The scale-1 improvement is meaningful despite the modest throughput gain: long-page band time falls from 22.0 to 4.7 ms, express median/p90 from 31/57 to 14/41 ms, and worst tracking lag from 294 to 131 ms. Verification found zero wrong frames out of 121; the stream found zero stale bands. That supports option 2 over option 4. (`evidence/round-notes-2026-11-04.txt`, lines 97–110.)

However, “pure gain” and “inert elsewhere” overstate the evidence. The engine switch retains tile read locks even when composition falls back to the GPU, forcing content-side double buffering. That fixes a demonstrated race, but it can increase memory use, allocation/copy work, and possibly paint stalls. The supplied evidence does not quantify those costs. Before shipping the default, I would check peak memory, buffer churn, and paint/slot-wait tails on eligible and fallback pages through the pending package/default validation. (`evidence/impl-review-findings-t505-cpubands.md`, F1; `evidence/pickup-top-section.md`, lines 61–64.)

Option 1 has a plausible mechanism, but the builder’s “Wikipedia’s gain without the paints’ price” remains a hypothesis. Wikipedia’s measured control read is 12.1 ms; removing it cannot be translated directly into delivered fps. With both switches, the reported 20.5 ms already includes about 15 ms of entry/parts preparation, while express median/p90 remains 65/285 ms. Paint sums rise from 3.2–3.9 to 5.1–5.3 seconds, no-slot ticks from 78–105 to 166–232, and bottom-band samples from 14 to 35. These show interacting limits, not proof that band composition alone controls throughput. (`evidence/round-notes-2026-11-04.txt`, lines 99–110 and 122–133.)

Nor does a band scaler address every important read: T-567’s slow first drags include **whole-frame reads after page changes**. Both cores were 94–95% busy. Moving bilinear sampling onto the CPU could reduce GPU contention while increasing CPU and memory contention; “80k pixels” is not a device timing. (`evidence/build-site-rows.md`, T-567 row.) Tile-edge sampling, fractional phase, alpha, and text consistency also expand the maintenance burden under R11.

Option 3 is weakest: fixing F5/F6 repairs zoom behavior but does not reverse the measured paint regression. Its supposedly cheaper paints premise was contradicted both here and in round 183. (`evidence/impl-review-findings-t505-cpubands.md`, F5/F6; `evidence/round-notes-2026-11-04.txt`, lines 122–130.)

Another scaler build/review/measurement cycle has a real usage cost; no numerical remaining budget is supplied. (`evidence/build-site-rows.md`, rows T-563–T-566.) R3 still requires 24 fps **with the card moving**; historical stationary-card results cannot establish compliance. (`evidence/kit-gpu-rendering-R3-R6-R12.md`, line 4.)

The strongest case against my pick is that dearer paints masked a real compositor benefit: unchanged fps around 10 does **not** disprove option 1. My second choice is therefore **1**. Repeated, interleaved Wikipedia rounds at resolution 1 would change my vote if a gated scaler improved delivered fps and express p90/bottom-band incidence, with measured scaling cost, unchanged paint/memory tails, and no tile seams or visible text shimmer against GPU references. That evidence would justify revisiting it; today, option 2 has the stronger benefit-to-risk case.
