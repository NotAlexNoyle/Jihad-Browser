of the card: the read's wait (36 / 11 ms), the copy from the base (13-16 ms), and Wikipedia's sidebars doubling
what is read. No stage is the main thread's or the page's script's. A simple page runs ~30 ms jobs (~33 fps
possible) but the GPU stalls in the tail bring it to 16.6; Wikipedia is 2.5x short of R3's 24.

(The no-frames runs' dumps looked at: real content, advancing down the page.)
- With nothing drawn by LunaSysMgr the daemon's own work makes desktop Wikipedia at 32 fps and the long page at 53:
  R3's 24 is met by the daemon alone.
- The card MOVING is what costs: the same work under a moving card runs at a third of the rate, every stage slower
  (the read's GPU wait, the copy, the compose) — the GPU and the memory shared with LunaSysMgr's redraw of the card.
  A still card that draws each new frame costs less (23.4 against 32).
- LunaSysMgr's CPU (the instance that draws the card) over the Wikipedia drags: 893 jiffies with frames to the card,
  408 with none (~33 ms of CPU per new frame drawn); 861 / 369 on the long page.
- The adapter's paint (render/adapter/BrowserAdapter.cpp, the GPU path's paint) wraps the 3.7 MB shared buffer in a
  new PGSurface on every paint and bit-blits from it: every scroll step of the card and every new frame. Whether
  Piranha uploads the whole wrapped surface each time is the next thing to find out.

The card's paint, measured (the owner: "possibly 1 or 3 but measure first to figure out which combination is best")
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The adapter's GPU-path paint timed while its paint log is on (render/adapter/BrowserAdapter.cpp, jihadPaintStat: a
"[paint-stat]" line every 2 s; the log's gate <state>/adapterlog, read once per LunaSysMgr process), by finger:
                          paints/s  new frames (blit)   repaints, same frame (blit)   the paint, % of a core
  desktop Wikipedia       18-23     120 (9.3 ms)        342 (8.6 ms)                  20-25 %
  the long page           18        227 (9.8 ms)        226 (8.9 ms)                  18 %
A paint is ~10 ms of LunaSysMgr's CPU, almost all PGContext::bitblt of the view (~720 kpx, 2.9 MB); the wrap is
0.02 ms. Most paints on Wikipedia repaint the frame the card already has (a scroll step).
- (1) The PGSurface kept for a frame across its repaints (<state>/pgcache): no change (blit 9.3 / 8.6 ms either way;
  9.4 against 9.7 fps, 16.9 against 18.5). The card's PGContext is built on Piranha's PSoftContext2D (libWebKitLuna's
  exports): a software blit that reads the whole source every time. Not kept.
- (3) The card scrolling a retained surface: there is no GPU-side card surface to scroll on this path; the card is a
  software buffer that LunaSysMgr takes to the GPU at each redraw, and a scroll step moves every pixel of it.
- The frame wrapped without alpha (<state>/opaque; the daemon's frames are opaque): the blit 9 -> 5 ms, the paint
  ~12 % of a core, LunaSysMgr's card instance -15 % CPU (921 -> 757 jiffies on Wikipedia, 865 -> 741 on the long
  page), the picture the same (looked at) — and the pan rate unchanged (10.0 / 15.7 fps against 9.7 / 18.5): the
  daemon's GPU waits do not come from the card's CPU blit but from what LunaSysMgr does on the GPU with the card's
  buffer at each redraw.

The sticky parts on the CPU (the owner's "both, parts first"), first rounds
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Engine 0070 (flag 128: a frame without its parts — every fixed and every sticky layer at opacity 0 in the composite
and in the diff step, no fixed rows, keeps or columns; the parts' rects still reported; a parts generation counted on
compositable updates under a fixed or sticky layer) and the daemon's JIHAD_GPU_CPU_PARTS=1 (the parts' picture with
flags 64|128; each content-only frame gets back what lay beneath the frame it was built on's parts and has the parts
drawn over it on the CPU; frames build only on their own kind).
- Scripted pan, desktop Wikipedia, the card moving (cp1): 14.0 fps against 8.6; the job 37 ms against 58, band reads
  18 against 31, no columns, no copy (own-slot frames). The mid-pan dump looked at: both sidebars once, sharp.
  But 10 pictures in the pan (the generation moves as Wikipedia repaints its contents panel) and 7 frames not shown.
- By finger (cp-wiki, cp-long): Wikipedia 10.2 fps against 9.7 (26 pictures, 19 frames not shown, 97 whole reads of
  71 ms); the long page 15.8 against 18.5 (1 picture, 57 whole reads). The whole reads are the kind changes: every
  picture made again sends the frames back to the engine's parts until it is in, and the main lane's frames between
  drags are of the other kind; each change is a whole read. Next: the main lane's frames of the same kind, and a
  change of the parts that costs less than a whole read (or the picture kept while the next is made, a correctness
  trade for the owner).

The parts on the CPU, made to pay (2026-10-26, afternoon)
---------------------------------------------------------
Four faults found by reading the finger rounds' logs, each fixed and measured:
1. The picture never matched (a loop): a frame without its parts has them at opacity 0, so the engine's keep test
   failed and it reported each part as its layer's whole rect; the picture (the parts shown) split the same layer into
   its covered pieces. Frame 2 rects, picture 3: every frame "a part the picture lacks", the picture made again at the
   same generation, forever. Engine: no keeps in a job without its parts (both report whole layer rects). Daemon: three
   misses in a row turn the parts back to the engine's for the document; the miss logs both sets of rects.
2. The lanes made frames of two kinds: the main lane's frames carried the engine's parts, the express lane's the CPU's,
   so neither found a base of its kind (68 whole reads in a round). The main lane now makes frames without their parts
   too (its base rules by kind, the parts drawn at its poll under the frame lock, a miss dropped as an express-dropped
   frame; not its second job on the one in flight, whose frame is dressed on this thread while the compositor copies).
3. A frame built on its slot's own frame had no scroll compensation in the diff (`engineShift` is "copied from the
   newest" only): any layer transaction during a pan was the whole frame changed for it, and the other two slots then
   stale whole. Without parts those frames are rare on a page with fixed layers (the window path is barred); with parts
   they are the rule. Engine: the diff leaves the card's scroll out for a frame with no base to copy, and checks that the
   shift it measured is the card's since the last composite (a scroll the page made itself is the whole frame changed).
   Whole tree changes in a Wikipedia round 30 -> 9, rows read 96k -> 57k; the long page 32 -> 10, 90k -> 43k.
4. T-511's "own slot is cheap" rule took a slot stale WHOLE after a one-off whole change (each drag's settle, where the
   engine takes the scroll over: the recent changes are whole too), a whole read where the newest frame was fresh.
   Daemon: never a slot stale whole. Whole reads with no change 20 -> 11.
(Also: the picture's own geometry need not match the frame's overscan row or height; a picture reads only the rows
around the last one's parts, whole again if a part falls outside; JIHAD_GPU_REQLOG=1 names each composite request's
source, which showed the requests equal with and without parts and pointed at the diff.)

The switch JIHAD_GPU_CPU_PARTS_STALE=1 (the owner's trade, not on): while a changed part's picture is made (55-250 ms),
frames keep the picture before it (at most 600 ms behind) instead of going back to the engine's parts and back again.
On Wikipedia the contents panel's highlight changes about once a drag; with the switch its new highlight shows up to
one picture late.
